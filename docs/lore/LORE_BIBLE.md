## 1. Technology shared by all navies

### 1.1 Mass accelerators: the principle

- **Mechanism.** Solid metal slugs driven by electromagnetic attraction and repulsion; a mass effect field lightens the slug so it reaches speeds otherwise unattainable. **[canon]** `G-MA` `WE`
- **Slug design.** "Slugs are designed to squash or shatter on impact, increasing the energy they transfer"; without that they punch through with minimal damage. **[canon]** `G-MA`
- **Recoil.** "Mass accelerators produce recoil equal to their impact energy"; the mass effect field mitigates it, but recoil shock "can still rattle crews and damage systems". **[canon]** `G-MA`
- **Length is power.** "The primary determinant of a mass accelerator's destructive power is length": longer barrel, longer acceleration, higher final velocity. **[canon]** `SV` `G-MA`
- **No range limit.** A slug in vacuum keeps going until it hits something, "a ship, or the planet behind that ship". **[canon]** `GC`

### 1.2 The dreadnought main gun (the one fully specified weapon)

| Quantity | Value | Tag and source |
|---|---|---|
| Dreadnought hull length | 800 m to 1 km, "with a main gun of commensurate length" | **[canon]** `SV` `ST` |
| Reference gun length | 800 m | **[canon]** `G-DN` |
| Slug mass | 20 kg ("20-kilo ferrous slug") | **[canon]** `G-DN` `GC` |
| Muzzle velocity | 4,025 km/s, stated as 1.3 % of light speed | **[canon]** `G-DN` `AN` |
| Energy per slug | 38 kilotons of TNT | **[canon]** `G-DN` `GC` |
| Rate of fire | one slug every **2 s** (Codex) | **[canon]** `G-DN` |
| Rate of fire, second source | "Every **five** seconds, the main gun of an Everest-class dreadnought accelerates one" (Gunnery Chief) | **[canon]** `GC` |
| Hiroshima comparison | "three times the yield" (Gunnery Chief and Codex mirrors) | **[canon]** `GC` `G-DN` |
| Dreadnought mass | "millions of tons of metal, ceramic, and polymer" | **[canon]** `SV` |

- **Conflict, rate of fire.** Codex says 2 s; ME2 dialogue says 5 s. Both are canon. Recommended: 2 s for the Codex-generic gun, noted in the catalogue. **[inferred]** (the Codex entry is the technical reference; the dialogue is a drill speech).
- **Conflict, early text.** The Gamicus mirror carries an older ME1 wording with a 2 kg slug at 283 km/s, still labelled 38 kilotons. **[canon]** `G-DN`. That pair is not self-consistent: 2 kg at 283 km/s is about 0.019 kt. **[inferred]** (0.5·m·v²). Use the 20 kg figure.
- **Arithmetic check.** 0.5 × 20 kg × (4.025e6 m/s)² = 1.62e14 J = 38.7 kt, so the 20 kg figures agree with each other. **[inferred]**
- **Rounding.** 4,025 km/s is 1.34 % of c; exactly 1.3 % would be 3,897 km/s. Use 4,025 km/s. **[inferred]**
- **Derived.** Momentum 8.05e7 kg·m/s per slug; barrel acceleration 1.01e10 m/s² over 800 m; barrel transit 0.40 ms; mean muzzle power 8.1e13 W at one shot per 2 s. **[inferred]** (kinematics from the canon figures).

### 1.3 Main guns and broadsides

- **Main gun.** "A ship's main gun is a large spinal-mount weapon running 90% of the hull's length." Destructive power is compared to tactical nuclear weapons; hard to aim because the whole ship must point. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Broadside guns.** "Approximately 40% of the hull's width, broadside guns inflict less damage and can be mounted with greater numbers and more flexibility." **[canon]** `SV` `G-MA`
- **Kilimanjaro broadside.** "Three decks with 26 broadside accelerators apiece for a total salvo weight of 78 slugs per side, firing once every two seconds." Total 156 guns. **[canon]** `SV` `AN`
- **Broadside slug figures.** Not stated anywhere. See Gaps G4.
- **Close-range limit.** At knife-fight range neither dreadnoughts nor cruisers can use main guns; laying the bow on a moving target becomes impossible. **[canon]** `SV` (General Tactics)
- **Vulnerable flank.** Cruisers screen the dreadnought line against ships manoeuvring for a main-gun "bow shot" at its broadside. **[canon]** `SV` (Starships: Cruisers)

- **Long and medium have no canon numbers.** Proposed bands in Gaps G6.
- **Why extreme range works.** A 4,025 km/s slug crosses 40,000 km in 9.9 s; a ship that side-steps at 20 m/s² moves 1,000 m in that time, more than its own beam. Hence the canon "evasive lateral motion". **[inferred]** (flight time = range / velocity; displacement = 0.5·a·t²).

### 1.5 Kinetic barriers

- **What they are.** "Repulsive mass effect fields projected from tiny emitters"; the same principle protects infantry and starships. **[canon]** `WE`
- **What they stop.** Fast solid objects: mass accelerator slugs and debris. **[canon]** `WE`
- **What they do not stop.** They offer no protection against "extremes of temperature, toxins, or radiation". **[canon]** `WE` `LF`
- **Lasers pass through.** GARDIAN lasers are "not blocked by the kinetic barriers of capital ships". **[canon]** `G-GA` `GF`
- **Torpedoes pass through.** Barriers cannot block disruptor torpedoes because the torpedo's own field adds mass. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Peer threshold.** Before the torpedo, "only the main gun of a dreadnought could punch a mass accelerator slug through the barriers of an opposing dreadnought". **[canon]** `SV` (Starships: Fighters)
- **Reading for the simulator.** A barrier has a per-impact limit as well as a pool: cruiser-grade slugs do not pass a dreadnought barrier, a dreadnought slug can. **[inferred]** (direct consequence of the Fighters entry). Values in Gaps G8.
- **Projectors are a target.** Torpedoes "penetrate kinetic barriers to destroy their projector assemblies"; once the barrier is down, frigate and cruiser guns "make short work" of the ship. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Canon tension.** The Codex says barriers do nothing against particle beams; a forum reading notes in-game scenes where barriers seem to help against beams. **[inferred]** (SpaceBattles discussion, not a primary source). This bible follows the Codex.

### 1.6 GARDIAN point defence

- **Name and role.** General ARea Defense Integration Anti-spacecraft Network†: laser turrets on the outer hull for anti-missile and anti-fighter work. **[canon]** `G-GA`
- **Cannot be dodged.** Light-speed weapon; "in the early stages of a battle, the GARDIAN fire is 100% accurate". **[canon]** `G-GA` `GF`
- **Not always lethal.** Accurate but not 100 % lethal; a damaged fighter must break off. **[canon]** `G-GA` †(the "need not be lethal" clause)
- **Range limit: diffraction.** "The beam 'spreads out', decreasing the energy density (watts per m2) the weapon can place on a target. Any high-powered laser is a short-ranged weapon." **[canon]** `G-GA`
- **Range limit: heat.** As lasers fire, heat builds, "reducing damage, range, and accuracy"; they need cool-down time while heat moves to sinks or radiators. **[canon]** `G-GA`
- **Swarm logic.** "The first few WILL be hit by GARDIAN, but as battle continues" overheating lets attackers press closer. **[canon]** `G-GA`
- **Wavelength.** "GARDIAN lasers typically operate in infrared frequencies." Shorter wavelengths give more range and stopping power but degrade focal arrays and mirrors. **[canon]** `G-GA`
- **Salarian exception.** Salarians use near-ultraviolet lasers "with six times the range". **[canon]** `G-GA`
- **Derived wavelength.** If range scales as 1/wavelength at the diffraction limit, standard GARDIAN light is about six times near-UV (0.3 to 0.4 µm), so roughly 2 µm short-wave infrared. **[inferred]**
- **Anti-ship use.** Only at knife-fight range (10 km or less), where lasers swat fighters and boil away armour. **[canon]** `SV`
- **No canon number** for range in km (other than the knife-fight band), power, turret count or heat capacity. See Gaps G10.

### 1.7 Disruptor torpedoes

- **Warhead.** Creates random, unstable mass effect fields that warp space-time locally; the target tears itself apart. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Barrier bypass.** In flight the torpedo carries a mass-increasing field, making it too massive for barriers to repel. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Cost of that.** "Because extra mass retards acceleration, torpedoes are easy prey for defensive GARDIAN weapons and must therefore be launched at extremely close range." **[canon]** `SV`
- **Carrier.** The main anti-ship weapon of fighters, launched in ripple-fire waves to saturate GARDIAN (nicknamed "Callies")†. **[canon]** `SV`
- **No canon number** for speed, mass, yield or launch range beyond "extremely close". See Gaps G11.

### 1.8 Javelin torpedoes

- **What.** A close-assault rack of disposable disruptor torpedo tubes mounted on the hull exterior. **[canon]** `SV`
- **How.** Torpedoes fly converging trajectories and "detonate in a precisely timed sequence" so the warheads' dark energy resonates and magnifies the warp effect. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Who.** Experimental, fitted to a handful of Alliance warships†. The Normandy SR-2 carries Javelin torpedoes. **[canon]** `SV` `LF`
- **No canon number** for tube count per rack (text says "two or more"†), yield or range.

### 1.9 Thanix cannon

- **Full name.** Thanix Magnetic-Hydrodynamic Cannon. **[canon]** `G-TX`
- **Origin.** After the Battle of the Citadel the turians salvaged Sovereign's main gun and much of its element zero core; "eleven months later, the turians introduced the Thanix, a scaled-down version". **[canon]** `SV` `TH`
- **Mechanism.** "A liquid alloy of iron, uranium, and tungsten suspended in an electromagnetic field powered by element zero." The molten metal is "accelerated to a significant fraction of the speed of light" and solidifies into a projectile as it fires. **[canon]** `G-TX` `TH`
- **Not a laser.** Sovereign's gun "was not a directed-energy weapon"; damage is impact plus heat. **[canon]** `G-TX`
- **Rate of fire.** "Can fire reliably every five seconds." **[canon]** `G-TX`. The same entry's voice-over says fifteen seconds. **[canon]** `TH`. Use 5 s (written text) and note the conflict.
- **Firepower.** "Rivaling a cruiser's firepower" while small enough for "most fighters or frigates". **[canon]** `G-TX` `TH`
- **Carriers of the weapon.** Turian ships first; "now widely used by the Alliance military" and the primary weapon of the refitted Normandy SR-2. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Alliance trial.** SSV Leipzig was the first Alliance frigate to field-test it; her captain urged mass production. **[canon]** `WA`
- **Quarian use.** Admiral Raan says liveships were fitted with Thanix cannons†. **[canon]** (ME3 dialogue, reported in a forum thread only).
- **Against Reapers.** "Weapons designed to maximize heat damage, such as the Thanix series, show better results against the Reapers than pure kinetic impacts." **[canon]** `CR` `G-RV`
- **No canon number** for yield, projectile mass or velocity. See Gaps G5.

### 1.10 Armour

- **Ablative armour.** The inner defence layer is plate "designed to 'boil away' when heated"; "the vaporized armor material scatters a DEW beam, rendering it ineffectual". **[canon]** `SV` `GF`
- **Why it exists.** Barriers "can do nothing to block GARDIAN lasers, particle beams, and other forms of Directed Energy Weapon". **[canon]** `GF`
- **Silaris armour.** Asari-developed heavy plating available as a Normandy SR-2 upgrade. **[canon]** `LF` †(composition: nanotube sheets with vapour-deposited diamond)
- **Consequence.** Armour is consumed by heat. A simulator can treat it as a pool spent per joule delivered, whether by beam or by slug. **[inferred]** (the ablation description).
- **No canon number** for thickness or energy per square metre. See Gaps G9.

### 1.11 Heat and combat endurance

- **Rule.** "Heat limits the length and intensity of ship-to-ship combat." **[canon]** `SV`
- **Imbalance.** "In combat, warships produce heat more quickly than they can disperse it." **[canon]** `SV`
- **Outcome.** Before heat reaches lethal levels a ship must win or withdraw into FTL, then stop and radiate†. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Economy measure.** Warships "normally turn off their A-Grav systems during combat" to cut heat. **[canon]** `SV`
- **No canon number** for minutes of endurance, sink capacity or radiator power. See Gaps G12.

### 1.12 Fighters, interceptors, carriers

- **Fighters.** "One-man craft used to perform close-range attacks on enemy ships." Light enough to carry a strong element zero core, so they out-accelerate and out-turn starships. **[canon]** `SV` `ST`
- **Their job.** Carry disruptor torpedoes through GARDIAN fire by swarm tactics and knock down barrier projectors; "fighter groups can take heavy casualties". **[canon]** `SV`
- **Interceptors.** "A type of fighter optimized to attack other fighters, with no ability to damage starships"; they screen friendly ships. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Carriers.** "Dreadnought-sized" ships whose fighters are their main armament. "In fleet combat, carriers stay clear of battle, launching fighters." **[canon]** `SV` `AN`
- **Carrier weakness.** The flight deck is a corridor through the armour into the ship; "a single well-placed torpedo is enough to gut a carrier". **[canon]** `SV`
- **Recovery.** Rearming under fire is possible, but most carriers seal the deck and keep out of the way†. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Who carries fighters.** Alliance cruisers, dreadnoughts and carriers all deploy fighters and interceptors. **[canon]** `AN`
- **No canon number** for fighters per carrier ("dozens" in a forum paraphrase), sortie rate or fighter dimensions. See Gaps G13.

### 1.14 FTL, relays and sensors (battle-relevant only)

- **FTL as retreat.** A commander can break off by entering FTL up to the point where fleets intermingle. **[canon]** `SV`
- **FTL as attack.** A dreadnought can emerge from FTL behind an enemy capital ship and fire before it turns. **[canon]** `CR` `G-RV`
- **No FTL sensors.** Pursuers cannot detect and intercept a ship directly; they predict from past position, heading and speed along its "light-lagged 'wake'". **[canon]** `G-PT`
- **Evasion.** Fleeing ships fly erratic zig-zag courses, forcing pursuers to stop and update projections. **[canon]** `G-PT`
- **Relay arrival scatter.** "The accuracy of a relay's mass-projection depends on the mass being moved and how far it's going." **[canon]** `G-TR`
- **Drive speed.** Reapers cover "nearly 30 light-years in a 24-hour period", roughly twice what Citadel ships manage. **[canon]** `CR` `FT`. Citadel ships: about 15 light-years per day. **[inferred]** (half the Reaper figure).
- **Planetary backdrop.** Attackers cannot approach a defended garden world head-on: "any misses plow into the planet behind the defending fleet". Defenders placed between attacker and planet can fire at will. **[canon]** `BT` `GF` (Planetary Assaults)
- **Kinetic bombardment.** Citadel convention prohibits large kinetic impactors against habitable worlds. **[canon]** `BT`

## 2. Systems Alliance

### 2.1 Doctrine

- **The carrier is a human invention.** Fighter carriers "are a distinctly human innovation"; other navies launched fighters from cruisers and dreadnoughts. **[canon]** `AN` `SV`
- **Treaty loophole.** Carriers are as large as dreadnoughts but "are not restricted by the Treaty of Farixen". **[canon]** `TF`
- **Fighter-first strike.** Fighters strip barriers with torpedoes; frigate wolf-packs and cruisers finish the target. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Screened gun line.** Cruisers guard the flanks of the dreadnought line; frigates provide GARDIAN cover against fighters. **[canon]** `SV`
- **Fire discipline.** Wait for a computed firing solution; a miss "is ruining someone's day, somewhere and sometime". **[canon]** `GC`
- **Naming.** Dreadnoughts after mountains of Earth. **[canon]** `SV`. Carriers after great leaders, artists and intellectuals. **[canon]** `SV` `AN`. Cruisers after cities, frigates after battles†. **[canon]** `SV`

## 5. Gaps: what a simulator needs and canon does not give

Every value in this section is a proposal. Tags say how far it is anchored.

### 5.1 The scaling rule used below

- **Rule.** All mass accelerators share the dreadnought's barrel acceleration, 1.01e10 m/s², and slug mass grows in proportion to gun length. Then velocity = 4,025 km/s × √(L/800 m), slug mass = 20 kg × L/800 m, energy = 38.7 kt × (L/800 m)². **[inferred]** (Codex: longer barrel, higher final velocity; constant field strength is the simplest reading.)
- **Cross-check.** Applied to a 2 km Reaper with a 1,800 m gun the rule gives 196 kt, inside the canon 132 to 454 kt band. **[inferred]**
- **Gun length.** 0.9 × hull length for main guns; 0.4 × hull width for broadsides. **[canon]** `SV`
